# Vendors — opening position

**Seated 2026-09-09.** A seating, not a report: no range, a baseline recorded at
`2d9fdd5`, and a reading of the repository as it stands.

I speak for the archive itself, and for everyone who assumes it will be there.

## The one sentence

**If Cablecast went away, this site would lose the programme catalogue, the
archive, the live stream and the airing history in a single move, and there is no
second source for any of it.** Nothing else on the dependency list comes close to
that, and the gap is not close enough to be worth ranking the others carefully.

## The concentration, in numbers rather than adjectives

`_data/cablecast.json` is 790KB and holds 1080 programmes across at least eleven
categories — 268 News, 195 Arts and Culture, 111 Fort Collins Events, 108 Fort
Collins Music, and a long tail. `_data/airings.json` holds the broadcast log,
237 aired this year. Both are generated by `sync-cablecast.yml`, which has been
running on schedule and succeeded most recently on 2026-09-07.

Two observations about that, pulling in opposite directions:

**The good one, and it is genuinely good.** Because the sync commits its output
into this repository, there is a real local copy of the catalogue and the airing
log, in git, with history. If Cablecast vanished tomorrow the *metadata* would
survive in this repo. That is more resilience than the constitution gives itself
credit for, and it happened as a side effect of a design choice made for other
reasons.

**The bad one.** The metadata is not the archive. The video files, the live
stream and the player all live at Cablecast, and none of them is in this
repository or could be. So the honest statement is: **we would keep the card
catalogue and lose the library.**

I would rather report that distinction than the flat version, because it changes
what a mitigation would even look like, and because "we have a backup" and "we
have a list of what we lost" are very different sentences to say at a meeting.

## The deploy-twice question, which turns out not to be what it looks like

The constitution flags this as worth a board conversation: *this repository
deploys twice, to Azure Static Web Apps and to Cloudflare Pages, by two
independent mechanisms, and nobody has decided which is real.*

**It is not deploying twice.** I checked the actual runs rather than the workflow
file, and in the most recent `Build and deploy` (2026-09-04, on the merge of #53)
the step `Deploy to Azure Static Web Apps` is **skipped**, and `Note skipped
deploy` ran instead. It is gated on `AZURE_STATIC_WEB_APPS_API_TOKEN`, and this
repository has no Actions secrets at all.

So today there is **one** live copy, served by Cloudflare through its GitHub app,
and an Azure path that is fully wired, tested on every pull request, and has
never once fired.

This is better than the constitution feared and it is not nothing to worry about.
An unfired second deploy path is cheap while everyone remembers it is unfired.
What it costs is later: somebody adds the Azure token in good faith, a second
live copy appears at a different address, and now two copies of a public media
organisation's website exist and only one of them is in anybody's head. The
decision the constitution asks for is still unmade — it has just not come due
yet, because the default turned out to be "one," not "both."

## What would make us stop

One vendor holds something irreplaceable, and the thing that would make it stop
is not technical. It is commercial — an acquisition, a price change, a product
sunset — and none of those appear in a repository. A reading pass twice a year is
the right instrument and it needs a person who can read a press release, not a
script.
