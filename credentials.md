## The datable one

The Slack invite in `_data/community.yml` is the worked example the constitution
predicted, and for once the git history answers the question exactly.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Link | `https://join.slack.com/t/fc-public-media/shared_invite/zt-48bxcjuc1-…` |
| Committed | **2026-08-28**, in `a30f919` ("Prune") |
| Shelf life | 30 days — the file says so, in a comment, beside the line |
| Therefore expires | **no later than 2026-09-27** |
| Regenerated by | nothing. The same comment says no CI is doing it |

The phrasing matters. The commit date is when the link entered the repository,
not when Slack minted it, so 2026-09-27 is a **ceiling** on its life, not a
prediction. It could die sooner. It cannot die later.

**It is not expired today.** That is a real and slightly unusual thing for me to
be able to report, and it is the reason this is worth raising now rather than in
October: the seat before mine would have found it already broken. I found it with
eighteen days left.

The consequence, in the constituency's words: anyone who clicks *Slack* on the
community page after the 27th gets an error page, the site keeps building, the
tests keep passing, and the person who finds out is a member who assumed they
were unwelcome.

A caveat I want on the record, because it would otherwise look like evidence: a
naive search says several of these were "last touched 2026-09-02." They were
not. That is the commit that added the inventory table to `ADVOCATE.md` — the
document *about* the credentials, not the credentials. **No rotation is
recorded for anything in this table.**

**And I want to say plainly that this is fine.** The oldest reference here is
about six weeks old. Nothing in this table is *overdue*; most rotation policies
would not have fired yet. Reporting these as neglected would be manufacturing an
alarm, and I decline to.

What is missing is not a rotation. It is **a rotation schedule and an owner** —
so that in a year, when these genuinely are old, somebody knows it without having
to run `git log -S` the way I just did.

## What I could not see, and will not try to

The repository itself has **no Actions secrets** — the API reports zero. The
organization's secrets I am not permitted to read (403), which is the correct
permission for me to have. So I cannot tell whether these credentials exist at
the org level or do not exist at all.

I am not going to chase that. My out-of-scope forbids reading any secret value,
and it is worth extending the spirit of it: I do not need to know whether a key
exists in order to ask who rotates it. The question survives either answer.

## What would make us stop

A deploy token that expires does not announce itself. The site simply stops
updating, and the person who notices is whoever eventually wonders why their
change never appeared — possibly weeks later, possibly a board member who
concludes the site is just wrong. The same is true of the GitHub App key and the
R2 credentials. Nothing here fails loudly. That is the whole reason this seat
exists.
